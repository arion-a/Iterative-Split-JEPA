* **What is needed for R1 to be a test:** colour and higher resolution (at least 64×64 RGB, ideally the paper's 128×128), a convolutional encoder, and the cross-view family as an invariance term in the encoder, as in the paper; or a CPU-feasible variant, 64×64 RGB with the red channel kept, as a registered follow-up. ManiSkill itself runs on the CPU here (software Vulkan), so the data is obtainable; it is the representation that is lacking.

## T1-T3: why the NUS ladder fails — three registered tests (2026-10-21, before any run; owner-requested; `configs/nus_t.yaml`, hash `configs/nus_t.criteria.sha256`)
**What is tested** (the three untested ideas listed after N): (T1) lags matched to the unknown frame rate, (T2) a representation that keeps slow content separate, (T3) more training data. On the N data (93 videos, same 65 / 9 / 19 split and test videos); all frames are re-decoded at 64×64 gray (`prep_nus64.py`); 32×32 inputs are made by 2×2 averaging of the 64×64 frames, so the baseline S0 is re-run through the same code and is expected to reproduce N approximately (not exactly).
**Design.** Five variants × 3 seeds: S0 (MLP, 32×32, base data = N), SH (S0 with half the training videos), SL (S0 with all frames: every full 660-frame segment of the 65 training videos, about 30 % more frames, each segment a release), C0 (conv encoder, 64×64, base data), CL (conv, 64×64, long data). Every variant is evaluated with two ladder lag sets, A = (8, 32) (as in N) and B = (1, 4, 16, 64) (a 4× geometric ladder covering 2.5 decades, since the frame rate is unknown), plus a label-free table of the best gap ratio of a single lag over 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128. Colour is **not** tested: the footage is white bars on black (gray only), so colour carries no information.
**Confounds stated in advance.** (i) The conv encoder is trained with 6000 steps at batch 128 (the MLP: 8000 at 256) because the CPU cost of the registered MLP budget for a conv net is about an hour per seed; a conv result is therefore conditional on that smaller budget. (ii) No new videos exist: the dataset's "train" shards repackage the same 80,193 frames without video labels, so "more data" can only mean more frames of the same 65 videos (SL), and the data-scaling direction is probed the other way (SH, half). (iii) Frame rate stays unknown; lag set B and the per-lag table are the empirical stand-in.
**Rule.** A (variant, lag set) cell *works* iff on all 3 seeds the ladder takes at least one cut with gap ratio ≥ 2.0, the minimum pairwise seed agreement on the test videos is ≥ 0.9, and the main coordinate's 8-step rollout NMSE ≤ 0.35, V ≤ 0.05, L ≤ 1.02. ICC ≥ 0.9 is reported, not required (it was never reachable on real video, see the Columbia energy ICC 0.553).
**Predictions (Claude's), each judged separately:** (T1) lag set B makes S0 work: expected **not** met (the lag-8 gap ratio on N sits at 1.99 / 2.003 / 2.36, near the criterion, so lags alone move it only by a seed-dependent amount; rollout error stays near 0.9). (T2) C0 works with lag set A or B: expected **not fully met**; I expect the gap ratio and rollout error to improve but not all of the three conditions on all seeds (my probability for "works" is about 25 %). (T3) SL works: expected **not** met; SH expected to be no better than S0 in seed agreement (data is not the binding constraint within +30 %).
**Decision rule:** T1 supported iff S0 cell B works; T2 supported iff C0 cell A or B works; T3 supported iff SL cell A or B works and SH's minimum seed agreement is lower than SL's; anything else is reported as not supported. Component numbers (cut ratios, agreement, rollout, V, L, ICC) are reported for every cell either way. Nothing tuned after seeing results.
