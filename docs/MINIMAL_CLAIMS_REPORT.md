## Reading
- **What holds, defensibly:** under the theory's own conditions (correspondence families that expose each layer, and a representation that keeps the layers) the iterated split identifies nested layers to depth 5, and it does so by correspondence, not by speed (E38, E39; with E21, E33, E34). A large gap with the block present was never wrong (31 of 31 here; 187 of 190 over earlier runs, the three misses before the gate existed). The gate keeps a wrong rung from damaging what the ladder keeps.
- **What the theory's training terms did not give:** E40 shows the invariance term alone does not force a nonlinear generic encoder to carry the fast, least-predictable layer. This matches the position of the question-ladder document: the encoder condition (A-ENC) is the central empirical bet, and here it fails for this objective and this encoder, in the one place the objective gave no incentive. The theory attributes the enforcement to the variance term (Theorem 4.2); an implementation of that term that acts on the first block's information, not only on marginal variance, is the untested piece.
- **Not claimed:** real images or video (A-ENC unverified there), pairs built from data beyond cluster limits (E31/E32), the n^(-1/2) rate with a learned encoder (established only in the linear core test), geometry and planning, a payoff test.

## Addendum 2026-10-22 (E42)
The fix for E40 (the variance hinge on each family's own block, my reading of Theorem 4.2's 'enforced by the variance term') moved the fast block from absent to partial (texture presence 0.27-0.52, from about 0) but did not reach 0.95 on any seed. The generic MLP with the prediction objective therefore does not meet the encoder condition (A-ENC) for the least predictable layer under either the invariance term or the invariance plus variance terms, at weight 1.0 and 10,000 steps. Not tried (stop rule): other weights, a whitened version of the hinge, longer training, other architectures.

## Addendum 2026-10-23 (E43 and the execution audit)
`docs/EXECUTION_AUDIT.md` lists the mistakes in my execution. The one that mattered: the invariance-trained encoders had dimensional collapse (E40 effective rank 9 of 12, E42 two dead directions), because the whitening constraint of Lemma 3.1 / Theorem 4.2 was not enforced. E43 enforced it (full-output Cov = I, and Cov of each family's own pair difference = 2I, weight 1, 30,000 steps). Result: **refuted as registered** (0 of 10 seeds reached presence 0.95 on every block), but the splits changed from failing to mostly clean: rung 1 is clean (angle under 5, gap over 10) on 9 of 10 seeds, both rungs on 7 of 10; texture presence 0.70-0.90 (from about 0); category presence fell to 0.76-0.99. Two things to decide: (1) the presence 0.95 bar (D15a, necessary condition) is contradicted by 17 clean rungs below it in this arm; (2) category presence dropped under the whitening terms on three seeds. Neither is tuned or re-run here.

## Addendum 2026-10-23 (E44, more rungs with the generic MLP)
The E43 recipe at 4 and 5 blocks (3 and 4 rungs), 10 seeds each: **confirmed as registered** (every rung clean on at least 7 of 10 seeds; by rung 9/8/9 at depth 4 and 9/9/7/7 at depth 5; every rung clean on 8 and 6 of 10 seeds). Failures cascade: after a failed rung the later rungs fail with gap about 1, and the gate flags them. Not shown by the pass line: the slowest block (the invariant core) is not kept (presence 0.56 at depth 4, 0.33 at depth 5) and the encoder has dead output directions (smallest covariance eigenvalue about 0 on essentially every run, against 0.91 in the three-block pilot). That is, the generic encoder keeps the split structure at depth but loses the one block the ladder ends on, which is the constant-across-the-video content the work is for. The mirror encoder keeps all of it (E34). Likely cause (untested): the whitening penalty's per-direction weight falls as 1/d, so it no longer prevents a dead direction at 16 and 20 outputs; recorded as execution item 9 in `docs/EXECUTION_AUDIT.md`, not fixed.
