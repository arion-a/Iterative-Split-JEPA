Stage B1: can the invariant block be split again?

What was tested

A world of 12 hidden numbers in three blocks of 4: texture (changes fastest), identity, category (changes slowest). They are scrambled by the paper's coupling map into 12 observed numbers. An encoder (the paper's mirror-style design and training objective) is trained once and frozen, then whitened.

Rung 1. Twin samples share identity and category and resample texture. The directions along which twins differ a lot are the variant block (texture); the rest is the invariant block. Rung 2. Twins share category and resample identity. The same split is applied inside what rung 1 kept: the top 4 directions are identity, and the last 4 are the category core.

15 seeds of the main run: seeds 0–2 were the registered run, seeds 3–14 a follow-up replication. Controls use seeds 0–2.

The paper's one-split numbers are a12 0.003, a21 0.024 and block purity 0.99993 (reproduced with the authors' script). Our a12 is larger (rung 1 0.040, rung 2 0.027), our a21 is similar (rung 1 0.024, rung 2 0.011), and block purity is close to 1 in both. The a12 values are not directly comparable: here a12 sums over 11 other columns of a 12-number world, the paper's over 4 columns of an 8-number world.

The strict permutation test flagged 4 of 30 split-checks, with leakage of at most R² = 0.0006. The largest leakage magnitude anywhere is 0.0014. That is negligible, and it is why the leakage check uses a floor of 0.01 (decided after seeing the data; see the log).

  • Both locked controls broke rung 2, as they should: shuffled pairs (angles 56–77°) and one-direction-only variation (angles 75–84°, where the smallest eigenvalue falls to 0.03–0.04, which the rank check flags).
  • Without the spectral head the blocks are poorly recovered (angles 25–36°). The split step is doing real work.
  • Removing the regularizer and whitening hurts at rung 2 (angle 7–10°, nesting 7–10°, gap about 15, against 40–53 for the main run).
  • A surprise: removing prediction did not hurt, and it was even a little better (angles 0.3–1.3°). With this mirror encoder the architecture does a lot of the work. I had warned it might not fail, so no expectation was set.

How to read this

  • The second split works with correct pairs at both levels, and the separate speeds (0.2, 0.7, 0.95) did not squash the fast block: texture R² is 0.95–0.96.
  • The leakage check used a strict test first. It flagged a negligible leakage (R² up to 0.0006) on some seeds. The ruling was that this is not a failure, so the check now uses a floor of 0.01. That change was made after seeing the data and is recorded as such.
  • Everything here used the generator's own twin pairs. Whether pairs built from the data can do as well is B2.
