Nested loss: what happened

E68 (hard world, 40 runs) and E71a (10, 12 and 15 rungs, 30 runs). All numbers come from the stored result files (results/nested_pod); 5 seeds per cell. The equations behind every number are in section 8.

Bottom line

  • Both registered tests failed. The nested loss did not give a clean block at every rung in either test (gaps below).
  • But the loss does split, and it splits by speed. In the mild world (E71a), the slow layers are recovered cleanly on all 5 seeds at all three depths, and the fast layers are not. The cut falls at about the same layer speed, rho about 0.86, whatever the depth.
  • The fast slices are not holding their layer and are not leaking into the slow ones (leakage at most 0.04 at 10 and 12 rungs). Their content has not been inspected.
  • In the hard world (E68) it does not work, even for the slow slices. Every slice gets worse as the coupling strength grows; the invariance itself is not achieved (residual 0.08 to 0.12 at strength 1, 0.66 to 0.79 at strength 8, limit 0.05).
  • The predictive loss tracks this: the further it sits above what a faithful embedding allows, the worse the presence (rank correlation -0.90 over 55 runs).
  • Not measured: whether the recovered blocks' speeds can be read back (nothing in these results does that).

1. What was tested

E71a: K = 11, 13, 16 layers (10, 12, 15 rungs), speeds rho = 1 - geomspace(0.8, 0.04, K), mild coupling (0.5). E68: 4 layers with speeds 0.2, 0.5, 0.75, 0.95, coupling strength 1, 2, 4, 8; arm A has every invariance term on from step 0, arm B switches them on one rung at a time. Pairs are exact (oracle). A rung is clean if presence is at least 0.9 (the layer is readable from its own slice), leakage at most 0.1 (no faster layer readable from it) and invariance residual at most 0.05 (the slower slices agree across the pair; two unmatched pairs would give about 2).

4. Is the invariance achieved, and is anything leaking?

At 10 and 12 rungs the slower slices agree across pairs almost everywhere (residual below about 0.09; most rungs below 0.05) and leakage stays at or below 0.04. At 15 rungs the first four rungs lose both: residual up to 0.20 against the 0.05 limit, and rungs 2 to 5 reach 0.13 mean leakage (worst seeds above the 0.1 limit). So the fast end gets worse with depth.

5. E68, the hard world: it does not work, even for the slow slices

Left: the invariance residual (limit 0.05) and right the final prediction loss against the reference, by coupling strength. Blue = arm A, purple = arm B.

Presence falls with strength for every slice, the slowest one included (0.95, 0.84, 0.71, 0.65 for arm A at strengths 1, 2, 4, 8). The residual (mean of the 3 rungs) rises from 0.10 to 0.71 for arm A. The curriculum (arm B) is not better: it is worse on slice 2 at every strength. Health stays at 0.78 or above, so this is not a collapse; the loss simply does not find the nested structure as the coupling gets harder.

6. How the predictive loss performs

For a faithful embedding (every layer kept, outputs whitened) the best prediction loss is the mean of 1 - rho squared over the layers: 0.561 for the 4-layer world, 0.412 / 0.410 / 0.408 for K = 11 / 13 / 16. The trained runs sit above it, by an amount that grows with hardness and depth:

7. What this says about the idea (my reading, flagged)

  • Supported (mild world, 10 to 15 rungs): minimizing the loss leaves the slow, consistent layers in the later slices, recovered cleanly on every seed, and the cut stays at the same speed as depth grows.
  • Not supported: a clean block at every rung (the registered test), and any recovery in the hard world.
  • Not measured: (i) whether a recovered block's rate (0.2, 0.6, ...) can be read back; (ii) what the discarded fast slices contain; (iii) whether the slow-rung result holds at a stronger coupling in the deep world (E71b was not run).

Limits: oracle nested pairs; block sizes given; one world family; 5 seeds; W = 1 in every world (the smaller candidate was healthy); steps 7,500 K (K = 11 to 16) and 30,000 (E68); the trained encoders were not saved, so these are summary measures only. The "reference" for the prediction loss assumes the outputs are whitened and every layer is kept.

8. The equations behind the numbers

Notation: K layers of 4 numbers each, so d = 4K numbers; z = [z1 | ... | zK] are the hidden numbers (zb has 4), z1 the fastest. Every formula below is the one the code ran.

A. From nothing to the input data

1. Hidden numbers over time (one chain per block b, the same speed ρb for its 4 numbers, unit variance at every step):

zt+1,b = ρb · zt,b + √(1 − ρb2) · εt,b,   ε ~ N(0, I4),   zt,b ~ N(0, I4)

Speeds: E68 uses ρ = 0.2, 0.5, 0.75, 0.95. E71a uses 1 − ρb = 0.8 · 0.05(b−1)/(K−1), i.e. ρb runs from 0.2 to 0.96.

2. What the encoder sees (the observation map, not trained): four alternating layers, then the observed numbers x ∈ Rd (as many as hidden numbers; no extra dimensions):

odd layers:  zR := zR + tanh(zL Wi);   even layers:  zL := zL + tanh(zR Wi);   Wi = s · Qi

zL, zR are the first and second half of the d numbers, Qi is a fixed random orthogonal matrix, s is the coupling strength (0.5 in E71a; 1, 2, 4, 8 in E68). Larger s bends the space more.

3. Matched pairs for rung k (u, v): they share every layer slower than k and redraw layers 1 to k:

u ~ N(0, Id);   vb = ub for b > k;   vb ~ N(0, I4) fresh for b ≤ k

B. The encoder and the loss

4. Encoder and predictor (both small MLPs, GELU): h = g(x) = W3·GELU(W2·GELU(W1x)), widths d → 16K → 16K → d. The output is cut into fixed slices h = [h1 | ... | hK], 4 numbers each; slice b is meant to hold layer b. The predictor f has the same shape.

5. Training loss for one step: N = 256 transitions (zt, zt+1), ht = g(C(zt)), ht+1 = g(C(zt+1)), and for each k = 1, ..., K−1 a batch of 256 rung-k pairs with outputs hu, hv:

L = Lpred + Σk=1K−1 [ 1 · Linv,k + W · Lwd,k ] + W · Lwh + 0.01 · Lvar + 0.04 · Lcov
Lpred = (1 / N d) Σ ‖ f(ht) − sg(ht+1) ‖2    (predictive loss: mean squared error over the batch and all d coordinates; sg = stop-gradient on the target)
Linv,k = mean over pairs and over the tail coordinates of ( huT − hvT )2,   Tk = slices k+1, ..., K    (the slower slices must agree across a rung-k pair)
Lwd,k = ‖ Cov( huk − hvk ) − 2 I4 ‖F2    (slice k must still vary across the pair: two independent unit-variance draws differ with covariance 2I)
Lwh = ‖ Cov(h) − Id ‖F2    (all outputs spread out, uncorrelated, unit variance)
Lvar = Σj max(0, 1 − √(Var(hj) + 10−4)),    Lcov = (1/d) Σi≠j Cov(h)ij2

Cov and Var are over the 512 encoded points of the batch (zt and zt+1 together). The weights 1, 0.01 and 0.04 are fixed; W is 1 or 10, picked per world by a 10,000-step pilot (the smaller one whose smallest eigenvalue of Cov(h) stays at least 0.5); it was 1 in every world. Optimizer AdamW, learning rate 3·10−3 for the first half of the steps, then cosine decay to 0; 7,500 K steps (E71a) or 30,000 (E68). Arm A has every k-term on from step 0; arm B switches the k-term on at step 5,000 (k − 1).

6. Reference for the predictive loss. If every layer is kept and each output coordinate has unit variance, the best one-step prediction of a coordinate of block b leaves variance 1 − ρb2 (from equation 1), so

Lpredref = (1/K) Σb ( 1 − ρb2 )    (0.561 for the E68 speeds; 0.412, 0.410, 0.408 for K = 11, 13, 16)

The reported predictive loss is Lpred at the last training step (one batch of 256 transitions), averaged over the 5 seeds; the reference assumes whitened outputs, so it is an approximate floor, not an exact one.

C. How the results are read out (fresh data; the trained encoder is frozen)

20,000 fresh hidden vectors z are drawn, passed through C and g to get raw outputs h (no whitening); the first 15,000 are used to fit the readouts, the last 5,000 to score them.

7. Presence and leakage. For a slice a and a layer c, fit a small network (tanh, 64 hidden units, 800 Adam steps, batches of 1,024; inputs and targets standardized) from ha to zc on the 15,000, then on the 5,000:

R2(zc from ha) = 1 − Σ ( zc − ẑc )2 / Σ ( zc − mean(zc) )2   (sums over test points and the 4 numbers of the layer)
presenceb = R2(zb from hb);    leakageb = maxc < b R2(zc from hb)   (b ≥ 2)

Presence 1 means layer b can be read perfectly from its own slice; 0 means not at all. Leakage is how well any faster layer can be read from the slice.

8. Invariance residual for rung k, on 5,000 fresh rung-k pairs, T = slices k+1 to K:

rk = mean( huT − hvT )2 / meanj∈T Var( huj )

0 = the slower slices are identical across the pair. For two unrelated points the numerator is 2 × the variance, so r = 2.

9. Health = λmin( Cov(h) ), the smallest eigenvalue of the output covariance on the 20,000 points: near 0 means an output direction is dead.

10. Clean rung and clean seed. Rung k (1 ≤ k < K) is clean if presencek ≥ 0.9, leakagek ≤ 0.1 (k ≥ 2) and rk ≤ 0.05. A seed is all-rungs-clean if every rung is clean and health ≥ 0.5; slice K (the core) is read only for presence. In E68 a seed is slice-clean if all presenceb ≥ 0.9, all leakage ≤ 0.1 and health ≥ 0.5. The clean-rung fraction at K = 16 is the mean over seeds of (clean rungs)/15.

11. The pair-difference spectrum (best-seed page). With 30,000 fresh points, the outputs are whitened (ZCA): r = C−1/2(h − mean(h)), C = Cov(h). With 30,000 rung-k pairs and D = r(u) − r(v), restricted to the directions Hk−1 not yet removed by earlier rungs:

Mk = Hk−1T [ (1/n) Σ D DT ] Hk−1;   eigenvalues λ1 ≥ λ2 ≥ ...

A direction the pair changes in has eigenvalue about 2 (unit-variance, independent redraw); a direction it leaves alone has eigenvalue near 0. Rung k changes layers 1 to k, so a clean nested split gives 4k large eigenvalues. The "ratio" column is the last large eigenvalue divided by the first small one.

12. Global R2 (best-seed page): a linear ridge fit from the 4K hidden numbers z to the whitened output r on the 15,000, scored on the 5,000: R2 = 1 − Σ(r − r̂)2 / Σ(r − mean r)2. It says how much of the embedding is a linear function of the hidden numbers.

13. Rank correlation between the predictive-loss excess and the mean presence over the 55 runs: the Pearson correlation of the ranks (Spearman), excess = Lpred − Lpredref, mean presence = (1/K) Σb presenceb.
