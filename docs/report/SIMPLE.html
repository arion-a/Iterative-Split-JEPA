Iterated splitting over 6 rungs

The question. A frame becomes a point in a space with many coordinates. The frame is built from layers of hidden numbers that change at different speeds. Can we split the space, layer by layer, from the fastest layer to the slowest, using only pairs of points?

The mechanism

  1. Pair. Two points that agree on every layer slower than the one we ask about, and differ in that layer (here the generator copies those layers, so the pairs are exact).
  2. Subtract. D = point 1 − point 2 for every pair. The differences stretch only in the directions that layer moves.
  3. Find the long axes. M = average of D × Dᵀ (a table of how the differences spread), then eigendecompose it: M = Σ λᵢ vᵢ vᵢᵀ. The directions with big λ are the block for that layer. gap = λ(last big) / λ(first small).
  4. Remove it and repeat on the directions that did not change, with pairs for the next layer. What is left at the end is the slowest layer.

Before step 1 the points are whitened (rescaled so no direction is stretched more than another). Nothing is trained after that.

Reading it. Every one of the 6 rungs finds its layer: angles of 0.3 – 2.2° (unrelated directions would give about 80°) and gaps of 72 – 137 (a clean cut needs more than 10). The error grows slightly with depth, from 0.4° at rung 1 to about 2° at rung 6, but stays small.
Conditions. This is the plain case: the pairs are exact, the bend between hidden numbers and points is mild, no encoder is trained (in this mild case training makes no difference, which I checked), and the data is generated by me. It is not shown to hold when pairs have to be built from the data, when the bend is strong, or on real images.
