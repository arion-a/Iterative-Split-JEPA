Stage B, maths-only check: the hidden cross-cutting factor shows up

Built a three-level world (texture, identity, category) plus one extra hidden factor that changes at every level. Ran two splits with no neural network and looked at what is left over after each split.
Step 16 · Stage B · 2026-10-13
What came out
Pass
  • The factor shows up as one leftover direction, the same one at both splits, with leftover size about 1.0 each time.
  • My detector's total (T) is 2.03 with the factor and about 0 without it; it separates the two cases on all 5 seeds.
  • The leftover direction sits within 1.6-2.2 degrees of the true factor at 40,000 pairs.
  • The three real levels are still recovered: 1.5 degrees at 40,000 pairs, 0.1-0.3 at 160,000 (plan threshold: 5).
  • Two of my own first expectations were wrong and corrected: I had assumed the levels would be recovered to 0.5 degrees (only true without the factor), and a 2-degree tolerance was too tight for this sample size.
Why it matters for the whole experiment
Shows the paper-style 'deviation map' idea works in the clean maths: the iterated split both recovers the hierarchy and flags a factor that does not belong to it. The next step is to see whether this survives a trained encoder and the rung-2 pair-building step.

Where our small gap comes from

Ran the five encoder variants from the plan (5 seeds each), plus the paper's baseline (no spectral head) with the mirror-style encoder.
Step 15 · Stage A · 2026-10-13
What came out
Pass
  • Starting point: ours 0.989 vs the authors' script 0.998 (gap 0.009).
  • 1. 8 observed numbers: invariant_r2 0.9900 ± 0.0023
  • 2. 10,000 steps: 0.9903 ± 0.0024
  • 3. both: 0.9916 ± 0.0023 (closes about 30% of the gap at most; the rule asked for half)
  • 4. paper's objective on our generic network: -0.0004 ± 0.0002 (collapsed; nothing recovered)
  • 5. mirror-style encoder + paper objective: invariant_r2 0.9976 ± 0.0018, a12_fro 0.0034 ± 0.0019, a21_fro 0.0231 ± 0.0021 (authors: 0.9980, 0.0034, 0.0233)
  • Paper's LeJEPA baseline (no spectral head) with our mirror encoder: invariant_r2 0.9615 ± 0.0325, a12_fro 0.331 ± 0.216 (authors: 0.9619, 0.329)
  • Rule set before running: a variant explains the gap if it closes at least half. Only variant 5 does (about 96%).
Why it matters for the whole experiment
Answers the open question from the paper comparison. Our pipeline is sound: with the same kind of encoder as the authors, we reproduce their numbers, including their baseline. The gap comes from our generic network having to learn the structure the mirror encoder is built with. Stage B will use the generic encoder (the realistic test); the mirror encoder stays as an 'easy mode' reference.

record #14 · 8fd4750d72
What the numbers mean (using the paper's names)
invariant_r2
How well the true invariant numbers can be read back from the invariant block (1 = perfectly). Paper: about 0.998
variant_r2
Same, for the variant block. Paper: about 0.997
global_r2
How well the whole representation is explained by the true hidden numbers. Paper: about 0.998
a12_fro / a21_fro
Cross-block leakage: how much the invariant block depends on the variant numbers, and the reverse. Smaller is better. Paper: 0.003 / 0.024
block_purity
Share of the learned map that stays inside the right blocks (1 = no mixing). Paper: about 0.99993
angle
Plan metric. How far the learned invariant direction is from the true one (0 degrees = exact).
gap ratio
Plan metric. How sharply 'changes a lot' separates from 'barely changes' in the matched-pair spectrum. About 1 = no split; above 10 = clean.
seed
One repeat with different random numbers. 5 seeds means it is not luck.
control
A run that is supposed to fail, to prove a pass means something.
